I'm Nathan. I'm drawn to the place where computation and mathematics meet: compiler
design, finite automata, models of computation, simulation. Working out why something
holds on paper, then writing the code that makes it hold on a machine, is the part I
actually enjoy - the theory is the point, and the program is how I find out whether I
understood it.

I'm currently pursuing a degree in computation and mathematics.

Alongside that I'm interested in philosophy and hacking, and I keep returning to the
same lineage: von Neumann and the machines he imagined, John Archibald Wheeler, and the
questions around von Neumann probes, robots and self-replicating systems - what it
means for a thing to build a copy of itself, and where that idea stops being a metaphor.

Some of that lands as code. A recent one is an error-correcting program built from
Reed-Solomon codes over GF(2^8): finite field arithmetic, generator polynomials and
encoding all work, with Berlekamp-Massey in place for the decoding side. Correction
isn't finished yet.

Most of what I know, I learned by building it. That habit is what this blog is: write-ups
where I work through a problem in public, including the parts that don't work the first time.
